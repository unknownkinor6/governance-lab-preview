Dedicated specialism

Point-of-care testing consultancy

Point-of-care testing (PoCT) brings results closer to the patient. Reliable services also need clear responsibilities, competent operators, quality assurance and effective oversight.

What I can review and support

The arrangements behind every result

PoCT governance covers people, devices, records and oversight. Work can focus on one area or look across all of them.

  1. 01Governance structures, responsibilities and device inventoriesWho is accountable, who does what, and which devices are in use where.
  2. 02Operator training, competency, authorisation and review arrangementsWho is trained, who is currently authorised, and when each review is due.
  3. 03Internal quality control and external quality assessment arrangementsHow checks are performed and recorded, and what happens when a check is unsuccessful.
  4. 04Policies, standard operating procedures and document controlWhether documents are current, controlled and workable in practice.
  5. 05Result recording, traceability, connectivity and information management processesHow results are recorded and linked to the patient, the operator and the device.
  6. 06Verification documentation, approval arrangements, audit and risk managementHow devices are approved and verified, and how risks are audited and managed.

Illustrative PoCT review

How a focused review could work

Illustrative example using fictional information. This is not a client case study and does not represent completed work or achieved outcomes.

The need

A fictional independent outpatient clinic wants a clearer view of the governance arrangements supporting its point-of-care blood glucose testing. Two meters are in use and 12 staff are listed as active operators.

The clinic needs to understand whether its records demonstrate current operator competency, appropriate responses to unsuccessful quality control checks and effective management oversight.

The proposed approach

A focused review would examine the agreed policies, device register, operator records, quality assurance records and governance reporting, supported by discussions with the clinical manager and PoCT lead.

In the fictional evidence set, four operator files lack a competency reassessment within the locally specified interval. Two unsuccessful quality control entries have no documented resolution, and routine governance reporting is not demonstrated.

Example deliverables

The example outputs comprise a findings report distinguishing evidence from uncertainty, a prioritised action plan with proposed owners and review dates, and recommendations for clearer authorisation, escalation and oversight.

Intended benefit and follow-up

The intended benefit would be a clearer understanding of the gaps and an agreed route to address them. Implementation and improvement would need to be verified through follow-up evidence. No reduction in risk, improvement in test accuracy or patient safety outcome is claimed for this fictional example.

Sample PoCT governance findings report

Fictional independent outpatient clinic · Point-of-care blood glucose testing

Purpose and scope

A fictional independent outpatient clinic uses two blood glucose meters across two clinical areas, with 12 staff listed as active operators. This sample considers accountability, operator competency and authorisation, quality control records and governance reporting.

Review approach

The fictional evidence set comprises one PoCT policy, one testing procedure, a device register, a training matrix, 12 operator files, 20 internal quality control entries, two external quality assessment rounds and three governance meeting records. Discussions with a clinical manager and PoCT lead are assumed for this scenario.

The review considers the agreed local policy and procedure, relevant device instructions and MHRA guidance. No direct observation, independent device testing or patient-record review is included. This is not an accreditation assessment or a determination of regulatory compliance.

F01
Operator competency and authorisation
Evidence
Eight of the 12 operator files contain a competency assessment within the review interval specified by the fictional local policy. Four contain initial training evidence but no reassessment within that interval. The training matrix does not clearly distinguish trained staff from currently authorised operators.
Finding and risk implication
The clinic cannot demonstrate current competency and authorisation for all listed operators. Missing documentation does not, by itself, establish that an operator lacks competence. Management may be unable to identify overdue assessments or restrictions requiring review.
Recommendation
Reconcile active operators with competency and authorisation records. The responsible clinical lead should promptly determine any interim supervision or restrictions required. Introduce a controlled authorisation register and monitoring of locally defined review dates.
