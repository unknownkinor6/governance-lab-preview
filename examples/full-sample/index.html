Sample PoCT governance findings report

Fictional independent outpatient clinic · Point-of-care blood glucose testing

Purpose and scope

A fictional independent outpatient clinic uses two blood glucose meters across two clinical areas, with 12 staff listed as active operators. This sample considers accountability, operator competency and authorisation, quality control records and governance reporting.

Review approach

The fictional evidence set comprises one PoCT policy, one testing procedure, a device register, a training matrix, 12 operator files, 20 internal quality control entries, two external quality assessment rounds and three governance meeting records. Discussions with a clinical manager and PoCT lead are assumed for this scenario.

The review considers the agreed local policy and procedure, relevant device instructions and MHRA guidance. No direct observation, independent device testing or patient-record review is included. This is not an accreditation assessment or a determination of regulatory compliance.

Executive summary

The illustrative evidence shows a designated PoCT lead, a register identifying both meters, an approved testing procedure and participation in external quality assessment. Gaps concern competency records, the documented response to unsuccessful quality control checks and routine oversight.

The proposed next step is a prompt management review of the evidence gaps, followed by an agreed improvement plan. These findings do not establish that incorrect patient results or patient harm occurred.

F01
Operator competency and authorisation
Evidence
Eight of the 12 operator files contain a competency assessment within the review interval specified by the fictional local policy. Four contain initial training evidence but no reassessment within that interval. The training matrix does not clearly distinguish trained staff from currently authorised operators.
Finding and risk implication
The clinic cannot demonstrate current competency and authorisation for all listed operators. Missing documentation does not, by itself, establish that an operator lacks competence. Management may be unable to identify overdue assessments or restrictions requiring review.
Recommendation
Reconcile active operators with competency and authorisation records. The responsible clinical lead should promptly determine any interim supervision or restrictions required. Introduce a controlled authorisation register and monitoring of locally defined review dates.

F02
Unsuccessful quality control checks
Evidence
Eighteen of 20 internal quality control entries are recorded as acceptable. Two are outside the acceptable range, with no documented investigation, corrective action or confirmation of an acceptable check before testing resumed. The procedure does not clearly identify whom to contact when a failed check remains unresolved.
Finding and risk implication
The records do not demonstrate how the two episodes were resolved or whether the conditions for resuming testing were met. This does not establish that patient testing occurred during either episode or that any result was incorrect.
Recommendation
Arrange prompt review by the responsible clinical lead, with laboratory or other appropriate specialist support. Establish the sequence of events and whether further assessment is needed. Clarify escalation and record the corrective action and basis for resuming testing.
F03
Routine governance oversight
Evidence
A PoCT lead is identified, but no deputy or documented allocation of oversight responsibilities is supplied. None of the three governance meeting records contains a standing PoCT update covering operator status, quality assurance issues and outstanding actions.
Finding and risk implication
The evidence does not demonstrate a consistent reporting process through which senior leaders can monitor PoCT assurance. Gaps may remain unresolved or depend on individual knowledge during staff absence.
Recommendation
Document responsibilities, deputy arrangements and escalation routes. Introduce a concise assurance report covering operator authorisation, quality control exceptions, external quality assessment, incidents and overdue actions.

Priorities and limitations

Management should first review F01 and F02 to determine whether immediate controls are required. The accompanying plan proposes owners and timescales for agreement. Completion dates must not delay action on an identified safety concern. Implementation and effectiveness require follow-up evidence.

Reference for the review approach

MHRA, Management and use of IVD point of care test devices. Consult current guidance and the applicable device instructions when applying an approach to a real service.

Action verification and closure

Operator assurance

Compare the active operator list with the approved register. Confirm that each operator meets locally defined competency and authorisation requirements or has an approved, documented restriction.

Quality control assurance

Review all unsuccessful checks since the revised process was introduced. Confirm that investigation, escalation and the basis for resuming testing are documented. If there have been no unsuccessful checks, use a documented scenario walkthrough to assess understanding; this does not replace later review of actual practice.

Governance oversight

Confirm that the assurance report has been discussed, exceptions assigned to named owners and overdue actions escalated.

Closure record

For each action, record the named owner, agreed date, status, progress, evidence reference, verification date and reviewer. Record the effectiveness review result and any residual concern or further action.

Do not close an action solely because a document has been written. Completion should be supported by the agreed evidence, with effectiveness checked at the planned follow-up point.
