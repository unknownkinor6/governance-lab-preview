Sample PoCT governance findings report

Fictional independent outpatient clinic · Point-of-care blood glucose testing

Purpose and scope

A fictional independent outpatient clinic uses two blood glucose meters across two clinical areas, with 12 staff listed as active operators. This sample considers accountability, operator competency and authorisation, quality control records and governance reporting.

Review approach

The fictional evidence set comprises one PoCT policy, one testing procedure, a device register, a training matrix, 12 operator files, 20 internal quality control entries, two external quality assessment rounds and three governance meeting records. Discussions with a clinical manager and PoCT lead are assumed for this scenario.

The review considers the agreed local policy and procedure, relevant device instructions and MHRA guidance. No direct observation, independent device testing or patient-record review is included. This is not an accreditation assessment or a determination of regulatory compliance.

F01
Operator competency and authorisation
Evidence
Eight of the 12 operator files contain a competency assessment within the review interval specified by the fictional local policy. Four contain initial training evidence but no reassessment within that interval. The training matrix does not clearly distinguish trained staff from currently authorised operators.
Finding and risk implication
The clinic cannot demonstrate current competency and authorisation for all listed operators. Missing documentation does not, by itself, establish that an operator lacks competence. Management may be unable to identify overdue assessments or restrictions requiring review.
Recommendation
Reconcile active operators with competency and authorisation records. The responsible clinical lead should promptly determine any interim supervision or restrictions required. Introduce a controlled authorisation register and monitoring of locally defined review dates.

How findings become actions, proposed responsibilities and completion evidence

Day 0 is the date management receives and reviews the findings. All actions are Proposed. Owners and targets require agreement; they are illustrative planning targets, not regulatory deadlines. Prompt clinical assessment takes precedence where a safety concern is identified.

How I work

A clear route from concern to action

Every project starts with a conversation and an agreed scope, so you know what you will receive, when, and at what cost.

  1. Initial discussion

    A conversation about your concern, your setting and what you need to know.

  2. Agreed scope

    Scope, deliverables, timescale and fee agreed before any work starts.

  3. Review

    Document review and/or on-site work, depending on the project.

  4. Findings and recommendations

    Evidence-based findings and prioritised recommendations in a written report or agreed outputs.

  5. Optional implementation support

    Help to put the plan into practice, with follow-up review where agreed.
